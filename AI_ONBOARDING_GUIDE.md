# Getting Started with AI — A Guide for Total Beginners

This guide gets you from "I've never used ChatGPT" to having an AI assistant that knows who you are, what you're working on, and can read your Gmail and Google Sheets. Most of it you can do by **talking to it in the car** — no typing needed.

There are three parts:

1. **Install the app** (5 minutes, do this before you start driving)
2. **The "get to know me" conversation** (20–40 minutes, perfect for a drive — you just talk)
3. **Connect Gmail and Google Sheets** (10 minutes, do this at home — it needs a Google sign-in screen)

---

## Part 1: Install ChatGPT (before you start driving)

1. Open the **App Store** (iPhone) or **Google Play** (Android).
2. Search for **ChatGPT** and install the official app made by **OpenAI** (the logo is a black-and-white knot/flower shape). There are lots of fake copycat apps — make sure the developer says *OpenAI*.
3. Open the app and **create an account**. Easiest option: tap **"Continue with Google"** and use your Gmail address. This matters later — use the same Google account you'll want it to read email from.
4. **Upgrade to ChatGPT Plus (about $20/month).** You can start free, but the paid plan is what unlocks the good voice mode, the smarter models, and the connectors to Gmail and Google Drive. If you're serious about this, just get it now — it's the whole point of Part 3. In the app: tap your profile/initials → **Upgrade**.
5. Find the **voice mode button**: on the main chat screen, look for the icon on the right side of the message box that looks like **sound waves / an audio waveform**. Tap it once now just to see it work, then hang up.

**Car safety, seriously:** set everything up — app open, voice mode started — *before* you pull out. Once voice mode is running you never need to touch the phone; it listens and talks like a phone call. Connect to your car's Bluetooth or CarPlay/Android Auto so it plays through the speakers. Never look at or touch the phone while driving.

## Part 2: The "Get to Know Me" conversation (do this in the car)

### Why you're doing this

ChatGPT has a **memory**. Things it learns about you in conversation get saved, and every future conversation starts with it already knowing your situation. A version of ChatGPT that knows your business, your family situation, your goals, and what you're bad at is *dramatically* more useful than a blank one. This drive is where you build that.

### How to start

Start voice mode (tap the waveform icon), and say something very close to this — you can paraphrase, the exact words don't matter:

> "I'm brand new to AI and this is my first real conversation with you. I want you to interview me so you can really get to know me and remember everything important. Ask me **one question at a time** — short questions — about who I am, my work, my family, my goals, my challenges, every account and tool I use, and how I want you to help me. **Quiz me like a consultant on their first day**: don't accept vague answers — keep asking follow-up questions until you have the specifics, real numbers, real names, real deadlines, and you understand exactly what I need. At the end I want you to save all of it to your memory. Let's start."

Then just answer its questions like you're talking to a smart new assistant on their first day. Interrupt it whenever you want — voice mode handles that fine.

### What to make sure gets covered

The interview will mostly steer itself, but before you end the drive, make sure you've told it about all of these. If it hasn't asked, just bring it up: *"Let me also tell you about..."*

- **The basics** — your name, where you live, family situation, how old your kids are, anything that shapes your week.
- **Your work** — what you do for money, what the business is, how it makes money, who your customers are, what a normal week looks like.
- **Your goals** — what you're trying to make happen in the next 3 months, the next year, and long-term. Be specific: numbers, dates, names.
- **Your challenges** — what's hard right now, what you keep putting off, where you're stuck, what you're bad at or hate doing. Don't be polite about it; the more honest you are, the more useful it gets.
- **Your tools** — that you use Gmail, Google Sheets, and whatever else (WhatsApp, QuickBooks, Amazon Seller Central, etc.). Name the specific spreadsheets you live in.
- **How you want it to talk to you** — e.g., "keep answers short," "explain things simply, I'm not technical," "push back on me when I'm wrong."

### How to end the conversation (important — don't skip)

Before you hang up, say:

> "Okay, we're done for now. Please do three things. First, summarize everything you've learned about me so I can hear if you got it right. Second, **save all of it to your memory** so you remember it in every future conversation. Third, tell me the top three ways you think you could help me this week."

Listen to the summary. If it got something wrong, correct it right there ("No, remember it this way instead...").

### Check that it actually saved (later, when parked)

Open the app → tap your profile → **Settings → Personalization → Memory → Manage**. You should see a list of saved facts about you. You can delete anything you don't want kept. If memory was off, turn it on and tell ChatGPT: *"Save everything from our interview conversation to memory."*

### Still driving? Put it to work right away

The interview isn't just a survey — everything it learned is fuel for real tasks. While you're still on the road, say:

> "Based on everything you've just learned about me, give me a list of five specific tasks you could take off my plate this week. Then let's do the first one together right now."

Or pick from these — each one uses something you told it during the interview:

- "Draft the email I've been avoiding — the one to [person I mentioned]."
- "Take the biggest goal I told you about and turn it into a week-by-week plan."
- "You know my challenges now — which one should I attack first, and how?"
- "Give me the five questions I should be asking my [accountant / supplier / landlord]."
- "Quiz me: what do you still not know about me that would help you help me?"

## Part 3: Connect Gmail and Google Sheets (at home, not driving)

This lets ChatGPT actually *read* your email and your spreadsheets when you ask it to — "summarize what came into my inbox today," "what does my sales tracker sheet say for last week." It needs a Google sign-in screen, so do it sitting down. Requires the Plus plan.

1. In the ChatGPT app (or at chatgpt.com in a browser, which is easier for this part), tap your profile → **Settings → Connectors** (sometimes called "Connected apps").
2. Find **Gmail** and tap **Connect**. A Google sign-in screen appears — choose your Gmail account and tap **Allow/Continue** on the permissions screen.
3. Do the same for **Google Drive** — this is the one that gives it access to your **Google Sheets** (Sheets files live in Drive).
4. If you see **Google Calendar** and **Google Contacts** connectors, connect those too — they make it much more useful ("what's my day look like?").

**Test it** with these, typed or spoken:

- "Search my Gmail for the last email from [a real person] and summarize it."
- "Open my Google Sheet called [exact name of a sheet you actually have] and tell me what's in it."
- "Summarize the important emails from my inbox in the last 3 days and tell me which ones need a reply."

If it says it can't access something, go back to Settings → Connectors and make sure the connection shows as active, and that you connected the *same* Google account you actually use.

**Juggling multiple accounts?** ChatGPT's connectors link one Google account at a time. If you run several Gmail addresses or businesses and want them all reachable and organized in one place, look at **Composio** (composio.dev) — a service built for connecting many accounts and apps to your AI. It's a more advanced step: get comfortable with one account first, then ask your AI to walk you through setting it up.

**A note on trust:** you're giving an app read access to your email. That's the trade for the usefulness. You can disconnect any connector at any time from the same Settings → Connectors screen, and you can delete memories any time. Don't connect a work account that has data your employer wouldn't want shared with a third-party service.

### Build your task & project log

One sheet, one job: nothing you care about gets lost. Every task and every long-term project goes in — and because the AI can now read your Sheets, it can pull them back up for you in the car.

1. In Google Sheets, create a spreadsheet named **Task & Project Log** with these columns: **Date · Task or project · Quick task or long-term · Next step · Status**.
2. Tell ChatGPT it exists: *"I keep a Google Sheet called Task & Project Log — that's where everything I need to do lives. Whenever I ask about my tasks or projects, read that sheet first."*
3. Make it a habit: any time a task or project pops into your head, add a row — ten seconds on your phone. If you're driving, just tell the AI about it and log it when you're parked.

Then, on your next drive, say:

> "Open my Task & Project Log sheet. Pick one or two projects I could actually move forward right now from the car, and let's work on them — or at least build out a framework for one, step by step."

## What to do in your first week

The habit that makes this stick: **when something comes up, ask the AI first.** Out loud, in the car, whenever. Some starters:

- "Draft a reply to that email from [name]. Keep it short and friendly."
- "I have to [thing you're dreading]. Break it into steps and tell me the first one."
- "Here's my situation... what am I not thinking of?"
- "Remember this:" — say this anytime you want it to keep a new fact about you.
- Once a week: "Based on everything you know about me, what should I focus on this week?"

The memory keeps growing every time you talk to it. The interview in Part 2 was just the starting file — keep adding to it.
